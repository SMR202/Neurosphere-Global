Neurosphere Global — Phase 1 Scope


Objective
NeuroSphere Global's objective is to advance neuroscience research and make it more accessible — bringing together a research team, ongoing projects, and practical resources under one platform, so that researchers, clinicians, and students working across EEG, neuroimaging, neurogenetics, and neuromodulation have a credible, central place to find guidance, data, and opportunities for collaboration.


Phase 1 Pages / Features


1. Home
Mission Statement
NeuroSphere Global brings together neuroscience research, practical resources, and open collaboration — for researchers, clinicians, and students working across EEG, neuroimaging, neurogenetics, and neuromodulation.
Introduction
NeuroSphere Global is a research group dedicated to advancing neuroscience through active research, practical guidance, and open collaboration, the team works across five core domains — neurophysiology/EEG, neuroimaging, neurogenetics, neuromodulation, and brain informatics/AI — to support researchers, clinicians, and students at every stage of their work.
This website serves as a central home for that work: a place to explore the team's ongoing projects, access curated research resources and datasets, learn about the people behind the research, and reach out directly for guidance or collaboration.

2. Research Guidance & Resources
This section brings together research information, references, tools, and guidance curated by the NeuroSphere Global team — organized around the group's core research domains, so researchers, clinicians, and students can quickly find material relevant to their own work.
Neurophysiology, EEG & EMG
Recommended reading, analysis tools, and guidance notes for EEG/EMG acquisition, artifact handling, and signal interpretation.
* Tools: EEGLAB, FieldTrip, MNE-Python, BrainVision Analyzer
* Guidance: 10-20 electrode placement systems, common artifact types and rejection strategies, quantitative EEG basics, needle EMG vs. nerve conduction study fundamentals
* Key journals: Clinical Neurophysiology, Journal of Neuroscience Methods
Neuroimaging
References and tool links for structural and functional MRI, fMRI, and DTI methodology and processing.
* Tools: SPM, FSL, FreeSurfer, AFNI, MRtrix3
* Guidance: structural vs. functional sequence selection, BOLD signal basics, DTI/tractography fundamentals, MRI safety screening considerations
* Data standards: BIDS (Brain Imaging Data Structure) for organizing and sharing imaging data
* Key journals: NeuroImage, Human Brain Mapping
Neurogenetics
Resources for variant interpretation, genetic counseling, and rare-disease knowledge bases relevant to neurogenetic conditions.
* Databases: ClinVar, OMIM, gnomAD, Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO), DECIPHER
* Guidance: variant classification frameworks (ACMG/AMP guidelines), referral pathways for genetic counseling, phenotype-genotype correlation basics
* Key journals: Genetics in Medicine, European Journal of Human Genetics
Neuromodulation (TMS/tDCS)
Protocol references and safety guidelines for non-invasive brain stimulation research.
* Guidance: published TMS safety consensus guidelines, tDCS dosing and safety considerations, coil positioning and motor threshold determination
* Trial registries: ClinicalTrials.gov, ISRCTN registry
* Key journals: Brain Stimulation, Clinical Neurophysiology
Brain Informatics & AI
Statistical and machine-learning resources relevant to neuroscience data analysis.
* Tools: Python (scikit-learn, PyTorch, TensorFlow), R
* Data repositories: OpenNeuro, PhysioNet
* Guidance: applying machine learning to neurophysiological and imaging data responsibly, avoiding overfitting in small clinical datasets
* Key journals: Journal of Neural Engineering, NeuroImage
General Research Guidance
Study design checklists, systematic review methodology (PRISMA), manuscript preparation notes, and a short FAQ for early-career researchers.
* Frameworks: PRISMA guidelines for systematic reviews, Cochrane Handbook, ICMJE authorship and manuscript recommendations, COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics) guidance
* Reference management: Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley
* Funding resources: KACST, Vision 2030 health-innovation funds, NIH, Wellcome Trust
* FAQ topics: choosing a study design, preparing a manuscript for submission, responding to reviewer comments, navigating authorship disputes

4. Activities / Projects
The research areas NeuroSphere Global is actively engaged in — spanning brain stimulation, genetics, imaging, and population health, all aimed at turning neuroscience discovery into real clinical impact.
Neuromodulation & Personalized Brain Stimulation
Exploring how brain stimulation techniques like TMS can be tailored to an individual's own brain activity, moving toward more precise, patient-specific therapy.
Rare Genetic Epilepsies & Neurogenetics
Studying the genetic and cellular basis of rare epilepsy syndromes, working toward faster, clearer diagnostic pathways for affected families.
Neurodevelopmental Disorders
Investigating new therapeutic approaches for conditions like Rett syndrome and related developmental disorders, with a focus on improving quality of life.
Neuroimaging & Treatment Safety
Using brain imaging to better understand how therapies interact with the developing and adult brain, supporting safer, more informed care.
Exercise, Cognition & Healthy Aging
Examining the relationship between physical activity, cognitive health, and the trajectory from healthy aging to neurodegenerative disease.
AI in Neurological Diagnosis
Applying artificial intelligence and machine learning to improve the speed and accuracy of diagnosis in complex neurological conditions.
Patient Experience & Health Systems
Understanding the real-world experience of patients navigating a neurological diagnosis, to help close gaps in clinical communication and support.
Regional Research Capacity Building
Strengthening research infrastructure and collaboration across the region's hospitals and institutions.
Interested in collaborating in any of these areas? Reach out — we're always open to connecting with researchers, clinicians, and institutions working on related questions.
